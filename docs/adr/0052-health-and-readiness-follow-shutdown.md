# Health and readiness are answered by the app, and readiness fails when Shutdown starts

`WithHealth(Health{Live: "/livez", Ready: "/readyz"})` answers two paths, given by the application (those two are the documented choice). Decided on 2026-11-01 for milestone 3 and built the same day (`health.go`, `health_test.go`).

- **Live** is 200 for as long as the app serves, Shutdown included. It says the process is not wedged; a liveness probe that failed during a drain would have the orchestrator kill the process before it finished.
- **Ready** is 200 while the app serves and wants traffic, and 503 problem+json ([ADR 0012](0012-client-errors-are-400-problem-json.md)) from the moment Shutdown starts, or while the application has said it is not ready with `app.SetReady(false)`: a cache still warming, a maintenance window.

Both are answered before app middleware, are left out of the API document and are not observed ([ADR 0051](0051-a-request-is-observed-when-it-starts-and-when-it-has-really-ended.md)): a probe every few seconds from each node is not traffic, an auth middleware would answer it 401, and a rate limiter would count it. The body of a 200 is `ok`, as text/plain. GET and HEAD are answered; another method on either path is a 405 problem+json with `Allow: GET, HEAD`. A field of `Health` left empty is not answered, so an app may serve only one of the two.

**Readiness checks no dependency.** Ready does not ping the database. When Postgres blips, a readiness that pings it takes every replica out of the balancer at once, and an outage of the database becomes an outage of the whole API, including the routes that do not touch it. An application that wants it anyway calls `SetReady` from its own check, knowing the cost.

**A delay before the listener closes.** `WithShutdownDelay(d)`, 0 by default, is an option of its own, so it also serves an app with no readiness route: Kubernetes removes an endpoint when the pod starts terminating, whatever readiness says. When it is set, Shutdown first fails readiness and keeps serving for `d` with the listener open; then it does what [ADR 0029](0029-shutdown-drains-and-then-closes.md) says. The delay exists because a balancer learns late: kube-proxy and the ingress apply an endpoint's removal some seconds after it happens, and an AWS target group notices a failed readiness only after its interval times its threshold (10 s with 5 s and 2). A listener closed at once refuses the connections the balancer still sends in that window, and those are errors the users see on every deploy.

**Refusals** (rows of `refusal_test.go`, built): a path that does not begin with `/`, or has a wildcard, a query or a fragment; the same path for Live and Ready; neither path; a path a route also registers, refused at that route's Registration naming both (a typed route, a group's route, a WebSocket route, the API document of `OpenAPI` and `ServeMetrics`, since all of them go through one function that adds a route); `WithHealth` or `WithShutdownDelay` given twice; a negative delay. Only the exact path is refused: a wildcard route that would also match `/livez` is not, and simply never sees it.

**What it costs.** When `WithHealth` is given, one comparison of the path's length against the two paths before app middleware, on every request: about a nanosecond, a claim (throughput and p99 are not measured yet and wait for the serial run of wave 2). A health answer is fixed bytes written into the engine's pooled buffer: **measured 1 allocation a probe, the request's context**, which the engine makes for every request before the app sees it, and nothing of the answer's own; a typed POST beside `WithHealth` stays at 2 (`TestAHealthProbeCostsOnlyTheContextAndARouteBesideItIsUnchanged`). The delay costs a deploy its length and nothing else. Memory per idle connection: none.

**As built.** `App.Shutdown` sets the draining flag first, so readiness fails before anything else happens, then, with a delay, waits on the app's clock and only then calls the engine's Shutdown ([ADR 0029](0029-shutdown-drains-and-then-closes.md)); the engine is not changed. The first Shutdown fixes the delay's length (the delay, or half of what is left of its context's deadline when that is shorter) and arms one timer; a second Shutdown waits for the same timer, or for its own context. `Close` during the delay ends it, and a Shutdown after `Close` does not wait. A test moves `bitetest.Clock` and nothing sleeps. Tests: `TestLiveAndReadyAre200OkAsTextWhileTheAppServes`, `TestReadyIs503ProblemJSONWhileSetReadyFalseHoldsAndLiveStays200`, `TestHealthIsAnsweredBeforeAppMiddlewareAndIsNotObserved`, `TestHealthPathsAreNotInTheAPIDocument`, `TestReadyFailsFromTheMomentShutdownStartsAndTheDelayKeepsServingUntilItEnds`, `TestTheShutdownDelayIsBoundedByHalfOfWhatIsLeftOfShutdownsContext`, `TestASecondShutdownDuringTheDelayWaitsForTheSameDelay`, `TestCloseDuringTheShutdownDelayEndsIt`. The context's deadline is read on the real clock, as the WebSocket spread reads it, while the delay runs on the app's.

**Scenarios it was put against.**

- _A rolling deploy on Kubernetes_ with `WithShutdownDelay(5*time.Second)` and a 30 s grace period: SIGTERM, readiness fails, the endpoint's removal propagates within the 5 s, new connections stop, and Shutdown drains with 25 s left, of which the WebSocket spread takes at most 3 s.
- _The same deploy without the delay_: the listener closes while kube-proxy still routes to it, and the refused connections are errors at the client. The deploy guide says to set the delay behind any balancer.
- _A hostile client_ polling `/readyz`: fixed bytes, no log, no allocation. It learns that a deploy is happening, which is not a secret worth a cost.
- _A database outage_: Ready stays 200, routes that query Postgres answer by their own errors, and routes that do not still work.
- _A balancer that reuses its connections_: served normally during the delay; after it, ADR 0029 closes the idle ones and marks the rest `Connection: close`.

**What it fails at.** A balancer that never probes readiness and never learns of termination still sends to a closed listener, and the delay helps only as far as its length matches the balancer's lag, which the application must know. A probe answered before middleware is not in the access log, so a failing probe is visible only at the orchestrator. `SetReady` is one flag: two parts of an application that each want the app unready must agree between themselves. Without the delay, the window in which a probe sees the 503 is the drain itself, during which the listener is already closed, so a balancer learns of the deploy only from refused connections; that is why the guide says to set the delay. A wildcard route that would match a health path is not refused and silently never sees it.

**What was rejected.** A readiness that runs registered checks (database, cache) in the core, for the cascade above. Readiness as a route the application registers itself: it works, but it sits behind app middleware and every app writes the same flag. Fixed paths: a platform that probes `/healthz` would need a proxy rule. The delay as a field of `Health`: it is needed without a readiness route too. Failing liveness during shutdown. A delay on by default: outside a balancer, and in every test, it waits for nothing. Answering `Connection: close` on every request during the delay: it would move a balancer's pooled connections off the instance sooner, at the cost of a burst of new handshakes on every deploy; a claim worth measuring, not a default to start with.

**Services around this order** (decided 2026-11-04, [ADR 0079](0079-services-start-after-the-app-binds-and-stop-in-reverse-around-shutdown.md)). A `Service`'s `BeforeShutdown` runs after readiness has failed and before the delay, sharing the first half of what is left of Shutdown's context, so the delay still takes at most half of the remainder.
