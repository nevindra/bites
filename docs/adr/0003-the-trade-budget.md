# Two guarded axes, and a feature wins over throughput within 10%

Performance is four axes, and they are not traded the same way.

- **Allocations per request** and **memory per idle connection** are guarded: each number in the table below is held by a test, so neither rises by accident, and a change that raises one states the new number, measured, and why it is worth it, in its row of this ADR. They are guarded because they compound with load and connection count, and because in Go an allocation is paid twice, once to make it and again in the collector. They are not goals: work to go below them waits for an application whose load needs it, because speed ranks last of the four principles ([ADR 0077](0077-four-principles-in-the-order-they-win.md)) and nilo, in Zig, serves the workloads that need the limit.
- **Throughput and p99 latency** are soft: a feature, or a change that makes the framework clearly easier or safer to use, may cost up to 10% of either, measured; past that its row says by how much and why.
- **Binary size** is recorded, and a change that adds to it says by how much.

A number in a document has a run behind it in `bench/result/`, saying what was run, on which machine, at which commit, with its spread. A number with no run behind it is a claim and is written as one.

Each row with a number is a test ([ADR 0033](0033-the-rules-are-build-steps.md)). The context is one allocation, not the two `context.WithCancel` costs, because the framework's context is its own type and implements `AfterFunc`; without that method every `context.WithTimeout` a handler derives from it would start a goroutine (`context.go:508-523` in Go 1.27). A done channel cannot be reused across requests, because a handler may keep its context ([ADR 0008](0008-no-pooled-object-reaches-the-user.md)).

A list cannot live in the slab: the slab is bytes, which the collector does not scan, so the pointers a list's elements hold (strings into the slab, nested lists) would not keep their targets alive. Pooling the backing array is ruled out by [ADR 0008](0008-no-pooled-object-reaches-the-user.md), since the handler owns the slice. So a list costs exactly one allocation, measured ([`bench/result/jsoncodec.md`](../../bench/result/jsoncodec.md)), and an empty or absent list costs none.

**What was rejected:**

- **Two hard axes** (the rule until 2026-11-04): allocations per request and memory per idle connection outranked every other concern, and either rose only with an ADR of its own naming the number. Replaced by [ADR 0077](0077-four-principles-in-the-order-they-win.md), which ranks breadth above speed: the numbers are kept and still tested, but a feature that raises one states it in its row instead of waiting for an ADR.
- **A flat target of one or two allocations for every request**, which the first version of this ADR's roadmap stated. It ignored the context that [ADR 0016](0016-the-handler-context-is-cancelled-on-disconnect.md) requires and the done channel a database driver creates; measured, `context.WithCancel` alone is two.

**Considered:** putting raw throughput first, as fasthttp and Fiber do. Rejected because the research ([`docs/research/`](../research/README.md)) shows the throughput the model allows is not where frameworks differ: a hand-written goroutine server, fasthttp and fib were within about 25% of each other, while the mistakes that cost users (pooled request data, double writes, a 500 for a client error) came from trading safety for speed.

**Idle WebSockets, built.** An idle WebSocket through the whole app (upgraded, its Socket and Inbox made, one message and its reply exchanged, nothing to read) costs **3,944 B of heap plus stack** (3,942 to 3,947 over three runs) and **4,911 B of RSS** (4,840 to 5,047) marginal from 10,000 to 20,000 sockets; without the message exchanged 3,841 and 4,628. The stack is 2,055 B of it, the goroutine's minimum. The research prototype of [ADR 0005](0005-our-own-http1-engine-with-a-goroutine-per-connection.md) held 2,947 B of heap plus stack and 3,615 B of RSS, so the built socket is about 1 KB heavier: the connection's state (the reader's header and mask, the wheel node, the queue and its mutex, the context, the Inbox's functions) is the difference, and it was not split further. The idle gate of a WebSocket is 4,700 B of heap plus stack, 20% over the measured number, as the other two gates are ([ADR 0039](0039-heartbeats-run-on-one-timer-wheel.md), [`docs/research/ws-gate/`](../research/ws-gate/report.md)). The plain and TLS gates were rerun with the WebSocket code in the engine: 3,294 to 3,640 B against 3,294 to 3,337 B before it, inside the spread of the run.

**Idle connections, parked (step 16).** With parking on, the default ([ADR 0005](0005-our-own-http1-engine-with-a-goroutine-per-connection.md)), an idle connection holds no goroutine, and its memory per idle connection, heap plus stack marginal from 10,000 to 20,000 connections over three interleaved runs, is **894 B plain** (890 to 896, gate 1,400 B), **7,581 B over TLS** (7,577 to 7,585, gate 9,100 B) and **1,642 B for a WebSocket** (1,632 to 1,661, gate 1,800 B), each gated by the idle tests' `parked` subtests. A connection of a listener with `ProxyProtocol()` ([ADR 0058](0058-the-proxy-protocol-is-turned-on-per-listener-and-accepted-only-from-a-trusted-proxy.md)) carries one more object, a 72-byte wrapper in the 80-byte size class, and the connection struct stays at 416 bytes, its size class, for every connection; measured with the idle test's 1,000-connection steps, each in a process of its own and interleaved three times, 1,177 to 1,292 B against 1,153 to 1,191 B plain, under the same 1,400 B gate (`TestTheMarginalHeapAndStackOfAnIdlePROXYProtocolConnectionStaysUnderItsCeiling`). `WithoutParking()` keeps the figures and gates above (3.2 KB and 4,500 B plain, 12.0 KB and 14,500 B TLS, 3.9 KB and 4,700 B WebSocket). The trade it makes: a request that finds its connection parked pays a wake, which in a scenario where every request does (2,000 connections, one request every 300 ms each) raises p99 from 72 to 101 µs, +40%, past the 10% this ADR allows p99; it is accepted for the memory axis, and busy connections keep their throughput, p99 and allocations within the spread ([`docs/research/parking/`](../research/parking/report.md)).
