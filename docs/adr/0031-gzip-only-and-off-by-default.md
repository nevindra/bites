# Response compression is the engine's and off by default, with gzip in the core and other encoders from modules

**Built** (milestone 2, step 15; the seam revised in milestone 3, wave 2, track E): `WithGzip(Gzip{MinSize})` and `WithCompression(Compression{MinSize, Encoders})` on the app, `internal/engine/encode.go` and `internal/engine/gzip.go` for the rest. What follows is the rule as built; the numbers are those measured on the built engine in [`docs/research/gzip/`](../research/gzip/report.md).

Response compression is a step of the engine's write, enabled on the app and negotiated by `Accept-Encoding`; it is off by default because the reference workload ([ADR 0002](0002-the-reference-workload.md)) usually sits behind a load balancer that compresses. **The engine compresses with an `Encoder` the app registered**: gzip from `compress/gzip` is the core's (`GzipEncoder()`), and zstd and brotli, which are not in the standard library, are the modules `modules/zstd` and `modules/brotli` outside the core ([ADR 0006](0006-no-third-party-dependency-in-the-core.md), [ADR 0070](0070-optional-features-are-modules-under-modules-each-with-its-own-go-mod.md)). Which one answers a request, the interface an encoder implements and the modules' levels are [ADR 0061](0061-compression-chooses-among-the-apps-encoders-by-the-clients-weights-then-the-apps-order.md): the client's weights choose and the app's order breaks a tie. `WithGzip(Gzip{MinSize})` is `WithCompression` with gzip alone. Everything below holds for every encoder; the numbers are gzip's.

**The numbers, and where it lives.** gzip's level is `BestSpeed`, fixed, with no level knob, and a body is compressed only if it is at least 1 KiB (`MinSize` of `Gzip` and `Compression` defaults to it, `MinCompressSize`; a value from 1 to 1,023 or a negative one makes `New` panic, a row of the refusal table, and `WithGzip` or `WithCompression` given twice, or both, panics too, naming both places). Measured on a synthetic JSON list (more repetitive than real data, so ratios are optimistic), BestSpeed took 3.5 µs at 1,087 B, 5.5 µs at 4,111 B, 13.4 µs at 16,468 B and 38.3 µs at 65,558 B, 1.4 to 2.8 times faster than the default level for 0 to 19% more output. Below about 1.2 KiB a response already fits one TCP segment, so compression saves bytes but not packets (claim, wire effect not measured). Writers are pooled in the core's gzip encoder (`sync.Pool`, 33.5 ns and 0 allocations for a get, reset, close and put, against 19 to 21 µs and 486 KB in 13 allocations for a new writer), so the 2-allocation row holds; the compressed output goes to one more pooled buffer of the engine, dropped past `maxPooled`, whatever the encoder. Compression runs on the finished response body after the handler returned, never blocks, so live writers number about `GOMAXPROCS` (16 writers about 8 MB at BestSpeed, a claim from that argument), not one per connection, and idle memory is untouched. Because a middleware sees only an opaque body ([ADR 0022](0022-middleware-sees-a-request-view-and-returns-a-response-value.md)), compression is a feature of the engine's write step, decided from the Response and `Accept-Encoding`; no writer, buffer or compressed slice reaches user code, and an `Encoder` is handed the engine's buffers on the condition `io.Writer` states, that it keeps neither ([ADR 0008](0008-no-pooled-object-reaches-the-user.md), [ADR 0009](0009-the-response-comes-only-from-the-return-value.md)). `compress/gzip` imports no `net/http`.

**The rules:** compress only when `Accept-Encoding` accepts a registered coding, and with the one it weighs highest, the app's first on a tie (RFC 9110: a coding with a weight above 0, or `*` with a weight above 0 when the coding is not listed; `gzip;q=0` refuses even beside `*`; `x-gzip` is gzip; a weight that does not parse, or has more than three decimals, is a refusal; several `Accept-Encoding` lines are one list; a refusal and an acceptance of a coding in different lines is a refusal; [ADR 0061](0061-compression-chooses-among-the-apps-encoders-by-the-clients-weights-then-the-apps-order.md)); skip a response that already has `Content-Encoding`, a 204 or 304, HEAD, a 206 or a `Content-Range`, an already compressed type (`image/*` except SVG, `video/*`, `audio/*`, `font/*`, zip, gzip, zstd, bzip2, xz, 7z, rar, pdf and jar), or `Cache-Control: no-transform`; always add `Vary: Accept-Encoding` once, also on a response not compressed for size, a 204, a 304, a HEAD and a Stream (not when the answer's own `Vary` already lists it or `*`); weaken a strong ETag to `W/` when, and only when, the body is compressed; never compress a `Stream`, SSE or `File` (they are written by the stream path, which only adds `Vary`); send the body as it is when compression would not make it smaller; `Content-Length` is the compressed length, and a HEAD, which is not compressed, keeps the length of the uncompressed body; and the guide documents BREACH (a response that reflects a secret next to attacker input).

**What a middleware sees.** The uncompressed answer: `Size()` is the body's length before compression and the headers `Content-Encoding` and the weakened ETag are added after the chain, so they are not visible there ([ADR 0022](0022-middleware-sees-a-request-view-and-returns-a-response-value.md)). The access log's `size` is therefore the uncompressed size.

**Built, measured** (Ryzen 7 9700X, Go 1.27.3, commit 6b086e4 plus the working tree, 5 runs, spread in the report): compression alone with a pooled writer and a reused buffer took 3.66 µs (3.66 to 3.78) at 1,087 B, 5.80 (5.73 to 5.82) at 4,111 B, 13.4 (13.2 to 13.6) at 16,468 B and 40.8 (40.3 to 41.7) at 65,558 B, 0 allocations, within 5% of the numbers above except 64 KiB (6.5% slower than 38.3). Through the engine on a kept-alive in-memory connection a compressed answer costs 4.4, 6.6, 14.0 and 37.8 µs more than the same answer plain, in 2 allocations up to 16 KiB, the same as plain; the wire carries 413 B for 1,220, 769 for 4,244, 2,140 for 16,602 and 6,996 for 65,692. **The 2-allocation row holds with gzip off, with gzip on and a client that refuses it, with a body under `MinSize`, and with a compressed body** (`budget_test.go`). A body over 64 KiB (`maxPooled`) is already dropped from the pool after the request whatever gzip does, so a 64 KiB answer is outside the row either way (5 allocations plain, 3 compressed, not investigated). Idle memory is untouched by construction (the pools hold no connection); it was not measured with gzip on.

**Cost against the budget.** 13.4 µs at 16 KiB is three to four times the CPU of a typical request, so it is off by default and paid per compressed response, not hidden in the 10% of [ADR 0003](0003-the-trade-budget.md); zstd and brotli as the modules set them cost 0.8 to 1.14 times gzip's CPU, and their typed answer is 2 allocations as well ([ADR 0061](0061-compression-chooses-among-the-apps-encoders-by-the-clients-weights-then-the-apps-order.md)). A panic in an encoder is recovered and the answer sent uncompressed ([ADR 0028](0028-panics-are-recovered-by-the-engine.md)). Evidence: [`docs/research/decisions-bodies/`](../research/decisions-bodies/report.md), section gzip, and [`docs/research/encoders/`](../research/encoders/bench-output.txt). **What it fails at:** streams and Range are not compressed; an app behind a compressing load balancer should leave it off, which is the default.

**What was rejected.** gzip only, the rule of milestone 2 ("the first version compresses with `compress/gzip` only"): it was the standard library's one coding, and zstd and brotli waited for modules. Measured in milestone 3, zstd and brotli at the levels the modules use make answers 8 to 42% smaller than gzip at about its CPU ([ADR 0061](0061-compression-chooses-among-the-apps-encoders-by-the-clients-weights-then-the-apps-order.md)), and every current browser sends both, so the engine's decision became a choice among encoders instead of a yes or no for gzip. The engine's own pool of gzip writers moved into the core's gzip encoder, behind the same interface the modules implement, so the engine pools only the output buffer. `MinGzipSize` became `MinCompressSize`, the floor of both options.
