# A job queue in Postgres is a module whose jobs are claimed under a lease and delivered at least once

An application that stores its data in Postgres has work to do after a request has been answered: send a mail, extract the text of an upload, scan it, call a webhook, push a notification. Until now each such job was a hand-made table with its own lease columns, its own `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` claim, its own retry counter and its own back-off, run by an `Every` that polls it. Athena Workspace, the first application on bites, has about twenty of them (`drive_texts`, `drive_scans`, `mail_outbox`, `push_outbox`, `doc_imports`, link previews), each a little different and each tested on its own. `Every` and `Cron` ([ADR 0079](0079-services-start-after-the-app-binds-and-stop-in-reverse-around-shutdown.md), [ADR 0092](0092-a-cron-schedule-is-a-service-read-in-a-given-zone-and-a-job-lock-lets-one-instance-run-each-occurrence.md)) run a function; they do not keep a job that must survive a crash, be retried, or be enqueued in the transaction of the change it belongs to. Decided and built on 2026-11-24 (`modules/pgjobs`, and `Broker.PublishTx` in `modules/pgnotify`). It is the queue River (riverqueue.com) is for Go, made smaller and on bites' own terms: a module with its own `go.mod` ([ADR 0070](0070-optional-features-are-modules-under-modules-each-with-its-own-go-mod.md)), refusals at Registration that name the line ([ADR 0007](0007-mistakes-are-refused-at-registration.md)), a `Service` for its life ([ADR 0079](0079-services-start-after-the-app-binds-and-stop-in-reverse-around-shutdown.md)), and nothing done in silence ([ADR 0077](0077-four-principles-in-the-order-they-win.md)).

**Decision.**

- **A kind is a Go type for its arguments and a handler, and the `Kind` it returns is the only way to enqueue it.** `pgjobs.Register[T](c, name, handle, opts...) *Kind[T]`, with `handle func(ctx, Job[T]) error`. `Kind[T].Enqueue(ctx, q, args T, opts...)` takes arguments of the kind's type, so a job of a kind that does not exist, or with arguments of another type, does not compile; there is no `interface{}` at the call site and no lookup by name or type at run time. The arguments are JSON (`encoding/json/v2`) in a `jsonb` column and decoded into `T` for each attempt. Register refuses, naming the line, a name that is not a plain name, a second kind of one name, a nil handler, a `T` whose zero value does not encode, a queue that is not declared, a negative option, two options values, and a Register after the Service started.
- **Enqueue writes through the `Querier` it is given: the caller's `pgx.Tx`, so the job exists if and only if the caller's change commits, or the pool for a job on its own.** One method for both, since a `pgx.Tx` and a `*pgxpool.Pool` both have `Exec` and `QueryRow`. This departs from `pgsession.WithTx`, which carries the transaction in the `ctx` ([ADR 0082](0082-a-postgres-session-store-is-a-module.md)): there the store implements a core interface whose signature has no room for it, and here nothing forces that, so the transaction is in the signature, where an agent sees it, and a forgotten one is not a silent write outside the transaction.
- **A job is in one of five states.** `available` (due, or waiting for its time: a delay, a back-off, a snooze), `running` (a worker holds its lease), `completed`, `failed` (its attempts are spent or the handler returned `Fail`, kept with its last error) and `cancelled`. There is no `scheduled` or `retryable` state with a process that promotes it: the claim's index has the run time in it, so an available job that is not yet due is skipped by the index scan.
- **A claim is a lease, extended by heartbeat, and fenced by a claim token.** The Service claims up to its free workers of a queue with one `UPDATE ... WHERE id IN (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT n)`, which counts the attempt, writes `lease_until` (the Client's clock plus `Lease`, 30 s by default) and a token new to that claim. While a job runs, one statement every third of the lease extends the lease of every job the instance runs. Every write that ends an attempt is guarded by the token (`WHERE id = $1 AND claim = $2`), so a worker that lost the job (cancelled, or taken by another after its lease ran out) writes nothing. Every `PollEvery` each instance takes back the jobs whose lease ran out: available again, or failed when that was the last attempt, logged at Warn with the reason in `last_error`. A heartbeat that fails while a job's lease runs out cancels the job's `ctx` with `ErrLost`, since another worker may take it; the heartbeat also cancels a job whose row no longer carries its token, which is how a `Cancel` reaches a job running on another instance.
- **Delivery is at least once, and a handler must be idempotent.** A job is run again when its instance dies mid-run, when the statement that records its success fails, and when a stop could not wait for it. The documentation says so in the package doc, on `Register` and in the guide, and gives the handler `Job.ID` and `Job.ScheduledAt` to key an idempotent write on.
- **A unique key makes a second enqueue a no-op while a job of the same kind with that key is `available` or `running`**, and only then. A partial unique index on `(kind, unique_key) WHERE unique_key IS NOT NULL AND state IN ('available', 'running')` holds it across transactions and instances; the insert is `ON CONFLICT ... DO NOTHING`, and Enqueue returns the id of the job that holds the key with `Duplicate` set. A job waiting for its time or for a back-off holds its key; once it has completed, failed or been cancelled the key is free, so "extract the text of this file" can be asked again after the last extraction ended. A key is scoped to its kind.
- **An error is a failed attempt, tried again after a back-off; `Snooze(d)` and `Fail(err)` are the two other answers.** The default back-off is one second doubling to an hour, plus up to a fifth more at random; `KindOptions.Backoff` replaces it. After `MaxAttempts` (20 by default; per kind, and per enqueue) the job is kept as failed with its last error. `Snooze(d)` makes the job available after `d` and gives the attempt back; `Fail(err)` fails it at once. A panic is a failed attempt, logged with its stack. An attempt has a timeout (`KindOptions.Timeout`, one minute by default, never none) and its `ctx` is cancelled at it.
- **A new job wakes the workers of every instance through `pgnotify`, and polling makes it correct without.** With `Options.Notify`, Enqueue sends `pg_notify` through the same `Querier`, so the wake-up is delivered when the transaction commits and never for one that rolls back, and the Service subscribes to it on the Broker the application already runs. This needed a publish inside the caller's transaction, which `pgnotify` did not have; it gains `Broker.PublishTx` ([ADR 0081](0081-a-topic-reaches-other-instances-through-a-broker-and-postgres-is-a-module.md), revised in place), with `Publish`'s checks and no timeout of its own. Without a Broker, and between notifications, the Service claims every `PollEvery` (one second).
- **A periodic job is enqueued once per period across instances, with no leader, by a row per schedule.** `Kind.Every(name, interval, args)` counts periods from the zero time in UTC; `Kind.Cron(name, spec, loc, args)` runs a `bites.Cron` on each instance, with its spec, zone and daylight-saving rules. The instance that first sees a period writes it into the periodic table (`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (name) DO UPDATE ... WHERE period < EXCLUDED.period RETURNING`) in the transaction that enqueues the job, so only one instance enqueues each period and a crash between the two leaves neither. The job's unique key is the schedule's name and the period's Unix seconds, and its `ScheduledAt` is the period's start. A period in which every instance was stopped is enqueued once when the first comes back, and the ones before it are not.
- **The Service stops claiming at `BeforeShutdown`, waits for the running jobs at `Stop`, and puts back what it could not wait for.** `Stop` waits until two seconds before its context's deadline (at once under `Close`), cancels the jobs still running with `ErrStopping`, waits up to a second more, and makes available again, with the attempt given back, each job whose handler returned, so another instance takes it at once rather than after the lease. A handler that ignores its `ctx` keeps its job until the lease runs out, and its id is logged; it is not put back while it may still be running, which would run it twice at once.
- **Every time of the Client is on one clock, `Options.Clock`.** A run time, a lease, a back-off, a period and a retention are read on it, its timers run on it, and the timeout of an attempt does when it is set. This departs from the other Postgres modules, which read `bites.Now(ctx)`: a job's run time is written by one call (an Enqueue, on a request's `ctx`) and read by another (a claim, on the Service's), and a test that enqueues from `context.Background()` would write the real time while the worker reads the test's; one clock for the Client cannot disagree with itself. In a test it is the `bitetest.Clock` given to `bites.WithClock`; nil is the real clock.
- **`Work(ctx)` runs the due jobs on the caller's goroutine until none is due**, after taking back expired leases and enqueueing due periods, and attempts each job at most once a call. An application test enqueues through a request, calls `Work`, and reads what the job did, with no Service and no sleep; with `bitetest.Clock` it reaches a back-off or a delay with `Advance`.
- **What an operator page needs is five methods and one for the housekeeping**: `Counts` (jobs per queue and state), `List` (newest first, by state, queue and kind, paged by id), `Get`, `Retry` (due now with fresh attempts; refused for a running job and for a key another job now holds), `Cancel` (an available job at once, a running one through its `ctx`), and `Prune`, which the Service runs once a minute: completed jobs after `KeepCompleted` (a day), failed and cancelled ones after `KeepFailed` (a week), 1,000 rows a statement.
- **An `Observer` sees each attempt as a `bites.Observer` sees a request**: `Start(ctx, Record) ctx` before the handler and `End(ctx, Record, Outcome)` after the attempt is recorded, the first given outermost. Enqueue stores the traceparent of `bites.TraceFrom(ctx)`, and each attempt's `ctx` carries that trace with a span of its own (`bites.ContextWithTrace`), so a tracing Observer records the attempt as a child of the request that enqueued it, as the otel module does for a request ([ADR 0063](0063-opentelemetry-is-a-module-whose-observer-records-the-server-span-and-names-it-to-the-core.md)).
- **The schema is one table and the periodic table, shipped as `Schema()` and `CreateTable`** like the other Postgres modules. Its indexes are partial on the live states, so their size follows the jobs waiting and running and not the table: the claim `(queue, priority DESC, run_at, id) WHERE state = 'available'`, the running jobs `(queue) WHERE state = 'running'` (so the heartbeat, which changes only `lease_until`, changes no indexed column), the unique key above, and `(state, finished_at) WHERE finished_at IS NOT NULL` for the prune. An id is a UUIDv7 made in Go from the Client's clock (RFC 9562, with a twelve-bit fraction of the millisecond and a monotonic guard in the process), not `uuidv7()`, which needs Postgres 18.
- **The Postgres floor is the oldest version the community supports, as for pgx: 15 when this was written.** The SQL uses nothing newer than 9.5 (`SKIP LOCKED`, `ON CONFLICT` on a partial index, `jsonb`); it is tested on 18.7 only.

**Refusals** (panics naming the line, rows of the module's tests):

- `pgjobs.New: Pool is nil`, a `Table` that is not a plain identifier of at most 40 bytes (so `<table>_periodic_pkey` fits Postgres's 63), a queue name that is not a plain name or runs fewer than one job at once, a negative duration, `PollEvery` under 10 ms, `Lease` under one second, a nil Observer.
- `pgjobs.Register("send"): ...` for each mistake above.
- `pgjobs: Kind("report").Every("hourly"): ...` for an interval under `PollEvery`, a name taken by another schedule or not plain, arguments that do not encode, a schedule added after the start; `Kind.Cron` for the same and for what `bites.Cron` refuses (its message, with the line of the `Kind.Cron` call).
- Enqueue cannot be caught at Registration, since its options are values of a request; it returns an error and stores nothing for a nil `Querier`, `RunAt` and `Delay` together, a negative delay or attempts, a priority outside -100 to 100, a queue that is not declared, a unique key over 512 bytes or with a NUL, arguments over 1 MiB, and two options values.

**What it costs** ([ADR 0003](0003-the-trade-budget.md)). None of the four axes of the core moves: the core is not changed, and `pgnotify` gains a method. Per job the queue costs one insert (and the `pg_notify` with a Broker), one share of a claim statement and one statement that records the attempt; per instance one heartbeat every third of the lease while jobs run, a rescue probe and a periodic check every `PollEvery`, and a prune a minute. Measured on a Ryzen 7 9700X against Postgres 18.7 in Docker over loopback, with that server's `fsync=off` and `synchronous_commit=off` (so the commits are cheaper than on a durable server), six runs of 3,000 jobs each in two sessions: an Enqueue through the pool 27 to 31 µs (32,400 to 36,400 a second, one at a time), 46 to 54 µs with the wake-up; jobs already stored completed by one instance at 10,800 to 12,000 a second with one worker and 30,000 to 37,200 with eight. On a table of 1,000,000 completed and 1,000 available jobs the claim of ten is an index scan of the claim index, 16 shared buffers, 0.18 to 0.24 ms; `Counts` reads the whole table, 41 to 45 ms there. The runs, their plans and what they do not say are in [`docs/research/pgjobs/`](../research/pgjobs/README.md).

**Scenarios it was put against.**

- _A job enqueued in a transaction that rolls back_ (`TestAJobEnqueuedInATransactionRunsOnlyIfTheTransactionCommits`): never stored, never run, and its wake-up never sent (`TestANotificationWakesTheWorkersOfAnotherInstanceAtOnce`, which also shows a worker with a poll of an hour running a job within a second of the commit).
- _Two instances racing for one job_, thirty rounds (`TestTwoWorkersRacingForOneJobRunItOnce`), and two Services running 400 jobs with four workers each (`TestTwoInstancesRunningManyJobsRunEachOnce`): each job run once.
- _An instance killed with SIGKILL mid-job_ (`TestAWorkerThatDiesMidJobLeavesItToBeRunAgainOnceItsLeaseHasRunOut`, a child process the test kills): the job stays running while the lease holds, is not run by another instance in that time, and is run again within the next poll after, as attempt 2, with the reason in its last error. A lease that runs out on the last attempt fails the job (`TestALeaseThatRunsOutOnTheLastAttemptFailsTheJobNamingWhy`).
- _A job three and a half times longer than its lease_ while another instance polls every 20 ms (`TestTheHeartbeatKeepsAJobLongerThanItsLeaseFromASecondRun`): run once, its lease seen moving forward.
- _A shutdown with a job that does not finish_ (`TestStopPutsBackAJobThatDidNotFinishSoAnotherInstanceTakesItAtOnce`): `Shutdown` returns within its context, the job's `ctx` has the cause `ErrStopping`, it is available with its attempt given back, and another instance runs it at once, not a minute later when its lease would have run out; a job that finishes in time is waited for (`TestStopWaitsForARunningJobToFinish`).
- _A failing dependency_ (`TestAFailedAttemptIsTriedAgainAfterAnExponentialBackoffWithJitterOnTheTestClock`): four attempts at 1, 2, 4 and 8 s plus a fifth, none a millisecond early, on `bitetest.Clock`.
- _Eight transactions enqueueing one key at once_ (`TestAUniqueKeyHoldsAcrossTransactionsThatEnqueueAtOnce`): one job stored.
- _Two instances, one periodic schedule, twenty periods_ (`TestAPeriodicJobIsEnqueuedOncePerPeriodBetweenTwoInstances`), and a cron schedule on two started Services (`TestACronScheduleIsEnqueuedOncePerOccurrenceBetweenTwoInstances`): one job a period.
- _A rolling deploy that adds a kind_ (`TestAJobOfAKindThisInstanceDoesNotKnowIsLeftForOneThatDoes`): an old instance does not claim a job of a kind it does not know.

**What it fails at.**

- **At least once is not exactly once.** A handler whose effect happened but whose success was not recorded (a crash, a lost connection, a stop that could not wait) runs again. The module cannot complete a job in the transaction of the handler's own writes; that would make a job's effect on the same database exactly once, and is not built.
- **Times are read on each instance's clock**, as in the other Postgres modules: two instances whose clocks differ by a skew judge a lease and a run time apart by that skew. A lease of 30 s leaves room for any skew a working cluster has.
- **A job taken back after its lease is due at once**, with no back-off, so a job that crashes its instance is run again on the next one until its attempts are spent.
- **Priority is read before the run time**, so the claim's index scan passes over the not-yet-due jobs of a higher priority in the queue before it reaches the due ones of a lower one. Many delayed jobs at a high priority make the claim slower; none of the measured uses does that.
- **`Counts` reads the whole table**, 41 to 45 ms a million rows; `KeepCompleted` is what keeps it short.
- **The wake-up is a second statement**, and Postgres takes one lock for every transaction that notifies when it commits, so transactions that enqueue with a Broker commit one at a time at that point.
- **A worker limit is per instance**, not per application.
- **A unique key does nothing to the job that holds it.** A second enqueue is dropped, not merged: an outbox that adds to a pending job (Athena's `push_outbox`, which counts the messages that joined a pending push) still needs its own row.
- **There is no kind that must never run twice**, such as a mail whose sending cannot be told apart from its failure: such a job is failed, not run again, when its instance dies (`mail_outbox`). It is a kind option the module could have; today the handler records its own progress.
- **A stop that waits for a handler ignoring its `ctx`** leaves the job to its lease.

What was rejected, with the reason:

- **River as a dependency.** It brings its own driver layer, migrations tool, UI and job-args interface, and a second way to register work beside bites' Services; the module is a few hundred lines on pgx, which bites' Postgres modules already depend on.
- **Enqueue looked up by the type of the arguments** (`pgjobs.Enqueue(ctx, c, args)` with a `Kind() string` method on the type, River's shape). A kind that was never registered is found only when the enqueue runs, and the type carries a name it could get wrong. The `Kind` value is the registration, checked by the compiler.
- **The transaction in the `ctx`** (`WithTx`), for the reason above.
- **States `scheduled` and `retryable` promoted by a loop.** One more statement on every poll of every instance for what the claim's index already orders.
- **`uuidv7()` as the column default** (Postgres 18 and later, above the floor), **`gen_random_uuid()`** (a random id spreads the inserts over the whole primary key) and **a `bigint` identity** (the id would be guessable and not sortable by the time across tables).
- **A leader for periodic jobs**, by an advisory lock or by `JobLock`. A leader is a lease of its own to keep and lose; a `JobLock` is at most once by its contract ([ADR 0092](0092-a-cron-schedule-is-a-service-read-in-a-given-zone-and-a-job-lock-lets-one-instance-run-each-occurrence.md)), where the periodic row commits with the job it enqueues.
- **A unique key that holds in every state until the row is pruned.** A job that completed would block the same work being asked again for a day, and a retention would decide a uniqueness.
- **`bites.Now(ctx)` as the clock**, for the reason above.
- **A middleware that wraps the handler** instead of an Observer. It could change what the attempt returns; an Observer sees and does not decide, which is what logs, metrics and traces need, and is the shape the core already has.
- **A LISTEN connection of the module's own.** It would be a second reconnect loop beside `pgnotify`'s, and a second connection per instance; the application already runs a Broker for its Topics.
- **Putting back at shutdown a job whose handler has not returned.** Another instance would run it while the first still does.
- **An attempt with no timeout.** A handler that hangs would hold a worker for ever; a job that needs an hour says so.
